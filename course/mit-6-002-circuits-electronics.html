Overview

Lumped-element abstraction treats each component as a two-terminal black box.

Key ideas

  • KVL: voltages around a loop sum to zero.
  • KCL: currents into a node sum to zero.
  • Plus V-I relations, solves any linear circuit.

Worked example

Series loop with 9V source and two resistors: V1 + V2 = 9.

Common pitfalls

  • Forgetting KVL needs a closed loop.

Why it matters

When circuit size approaches signal wavelength, you need Maxwell.

Overview

Two powerful simplifications for linear circuits.

Key ideas

  • Superposition: sum of single-source responses.
  • Thevenin: Vth in series with Rth.
  • Norton: current source in parallel with R.

Worked example

Rth = Voc/Isc. Then any load sees a simple two-element source.

Common pitfalls

  • Applying to nonlinear circuits.

Why it matters

Reduces a complex circuit to two numbers.

Overview

RC and RL circuits respond with an exponential curve.

Key ideas

  • tau = RC or L/R.
  • After 1 tau, ~63% done.
  • After 5 tau, settled.

Worked example

10 kohm and 1 uF: tau = 10 ms. A 5V step reaches 3.16V after 10 ms.

Common pitfalls

  • Wrong sign in the exponential.

Why it matters

The time constant is the fundamental timescale.

Overview

RLC circuits are second-order.

Key ideas

  • omega_0 = 1/sqrt(LC).
  • zeta determines damping.
  • Underdamped: oscillations with decay.

Worked example

L=1mH, C=1uF: omega_0 ~ 31,623 rad/s.

Common pitfalls

  • Ignoring R controls damping.

Why it matters

Second-order behaviour is everywhere.

High-gain differential amplifier with feedback.

Key ideas

Overview

The MOSFET is a voltage-controlled switch.

Key ideas

  • Digital: on or off.
  • CMOS inverter: one PMOS, one NMOS.
  • NAND/NOR are the building blocks.

Worked example

CMOS inverter with input 0 outputs VDD.

Common pitfalls

  • Assuming perfect switches.

Why it matters

Every processor is billions of MOSFETs.
