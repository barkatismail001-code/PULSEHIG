Laptop Overheating and Throttling? Clean It, Undervolt It

👤 TechPulse Team 📅 2026-10-03 ⏱ 8 min read
🎯 Difficulty: Intermediate ⏱ Fix time: 45 min 💰 Cost: €5–15

A gaming laptop that used to hit 4.5 GHz now sits at 2.6 GHz under load with fans screaming. That is thermal throttling — the CPU is protecting itself. You can fix it without replacing the laptop.

1. Measure before you touch anything

Install HWiNFO64. Run a 10-minute stress test with Cinebench. Watch:

  • CPU Package Power — should stay near the TDP (e.g. 45 W). If it drops to 15 W, you are throttling.
  • Core Temperatures — above 95 °C means thermal limit.
  • Thermal Throttling flag — HWiNFO shows "Yes" when active.

2. Clean the fan and heatsink

Dust is the #1 cause. Open the back panel, remove the fan, and blow compressed air through the heatsink fins from the exhaust side. A clogged heatsink can raise temperatures by 20 °C.

3. Replace the thermal paste

Factory paste dries out in 2–3 years. Use Arctic MX-6 or Thermal Grizzly Kryonaut. On laptops with a bare die, use a thin line, not a pea.

4. Undervolt the CPU (Intel)

Download ThrottleStop. In the FIVR window:

CPU Core Offset: -100 mV
CPU Cache Offset: -100 mV
Apply → Save

Test with Cinebench. If it crashes, reduce to -80 mV. Most Intel chips handle -100 to -125 mV stable, dropping 8–12 °C.

5. Limit turbo boost (quick fix)

ThrottleStop → TPL → Turbo Boost Long Power Max: 35 W
Turbo Time Limit: 28 s

You lose 5% performance and gain 15 °C of headroom.

6. Repaste the GPU too

On gaming laptops the GPU shares the heatsink. Skipping it leaves half the cooling capacity unused.

7. Use a cooling pad correctly

A cooling pad only helps if the laptop's intake is on the bottom. If intakes are on the side, a pad does almost nothing — raise the rear edge instead.

Summary

Measure with HWiNFO, clean the fan, repaste CPU and GPU, undervolt by -100 mV, and cap turbo power. A throttling laptop typically regains 40–60% of its lost performance.
