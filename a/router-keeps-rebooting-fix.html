Router Keeps Rebooting? 6 Causes You Can Fix Today

👤 TechPulse Team 📅 2026-10-03 ⏱ 6 min read
🎯 Difficulty: Beginner ⏱ Fix time: 20 min 💰 Cost: Free–€20

A router that reboots itself is usually telling you the power supply is dying, the firmware is buggy, or the DHCP table is full. Only one of those requires buying a new router.

1. Check the power adapter

Routers ship with 9V/12V adapters that lose capacitance after 3–4 years. A failing adapter causes random reboots under load. Measure with a multimeter — if the voltage drops more than 5% under load, replace the adapter (€10).

2. Feel the router

If the case is too hot to touch, the SoC is thermally resetting. Move it out of a closed cabinet, raise it off the shelf, and blow dust out of the vents.

3. Update the firmware

Admin panel → Administration → Firmware Upgrade

TP-Link, Asus and Netgear all shipped firmware in 2024–2025 that fixed reboot loops under heavy NAT load.

4. Clear the DHCP lease table

A full lease table causes the router to restart its DHCP service.

Admin → DHCP → Lease Time → 86400 → Save → Reboot

5. Fix IP conflicts

A static IP inside the DHCP pool causes conflicts that crash some routers. Assign static IPs only outside the pool (e.g. 192.168.1.2–192.168.1.50 for static, .100–.200 for DHCP).

6. Test the ISP line

Check the router's log for PPPoE down events. If your line drops, the router reboots to renegotiate. Call your ISP with the log timestamps.

7. When to replace it

If the router is over 6 years old, has 8+ devices, and reboots daily even after all the above, replace it. A modern Wi-Fi 6 router consumes less power and handles 3× the clients.

Summary

Test the adapter, cool the router, update firmware, shorten the lease time, fix IP conflicts, and check the ISP log. That order solves 90% of reboot loops.
