PC Won't Boot? The 7-Step No-POST Checklist

👤 TechPulse Team 📅 2026-10-03 ⏱ 8 min read
🎯 Difficulty: Intermediate ⏱ Fix time: 30 min 💰 Cost: Free

The fans spin, the LEDs light up, but there is no display and no beep. Before you assume the motherboard is dead, strip the system down to the minimum and let it tell you what is wrong.

1. Check the obvious

  • Monitor input source correct? Try a different cable and port.
  • PSU switch on the back set to "I", not "O".
  • Front panel power button cable on the right header.

2. Listen for beep codes

If the board has a speaker, the beep pattern tells you the fault:

  • 1 long, 2 short — GPU error.
  • Continuous short — RAM not seated.
  • No beep, fans spin — CPU or board.

3. Reseat the RAM

Remove both sticks, blow out the slots, and reinsert with firm pressure until both clips click. Try one stick in slot A2 only. RAM not fully seated is the #1 cause of no-POST.

4. Clear CMOS

Unplug PSU → remove CR2032 battery → hold power button 10 s → wait 5 min → reinsert battery → boot

Or use the CLR_CMOS jumper if your board has one.

5. Minimum configuration

Disconnect everything except: CPU + cooler, 1 RAM stick, PSU, and the monitor on the integrated GPU (remove the discrete GPU). If it boots, add components back one at a time.

6. Paperclip-test the PSU

Disconnect PSU from motherboard.
Bridge the green wire (pin 16) and any black wire (pin 17) on the 24-pin connector.
Plug in and switch on — the fan should spin.

If it does not spin, the PSU is dead. If it spins but the PC does not POST, the PSU may still be weak under load — test with a known-good unit.

7. BIOS recovery

If the board has USB BIOS Flashback, put the BIOS file on a FAT32 USB stick and press the flashback button with the PC off. This recovers a corrupted BIOS after a failed update.

Summary

Reseat RAM, clear CMOS, strip to minimum config, and test the PSU. Those four steps identify 90% of no-POST causes without buying parts.
