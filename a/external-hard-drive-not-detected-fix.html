External Hard Drive Not Detected? Fix It Before You Panic

👤 TechPulse Team 📅 2026-10-03 ⏱ 6 min read
🎯 Difficulty: Beginner ⏱ Fix time: 15 min 💰 Cost: Free

The drive spins, the light blinks, but Windows does not show it. Before assuming the worst, work through these steps — most "dead" drives are only missing a drive letter or stuck in a RAW state.

1. Check Disk Management first

Win + X → Disk Management

If the drive appears with a black "Unallocated" bar or no letter, the hardware is fine. Skip to step 3.

2. Try a different port and cable

USB 3.0 ports on the front of a desktop are often under-powered. Use a rear port, and replace the cable — thin cables cause intermittent detection.

3. Assign a drive letter

Disk Management → right-click the partition → Change Drive Letter and Paths → Add → E:

Windows sometimes drops the letter after a reboot, especially for drives used on multiple PCs.

4. If the partition is RAW

Do not format. Install TestDisk and run:

sudo testdisk /dev/sdb
# Analyse → Quick Search → Write partition table

This rebuilds the partition table without touching the data.

5. Check power on 2.5" drives

A 2.5" drive needs ~900 mA. On a USB hub without external power, it will click and disconnect. Use a powered hub or a Y-cable.

6. Listen for clicking

If the drive clicks rhythmically, the heads are failing. Power it off immediately and send it to a data recovery lab — every extra spin reduces the chance of recovery.

7. Linux users

lsblk
sudo dmesg | tail -30
sudo mount /dev/sdb1 /mnt

If the kernel does not even list the device, it is a hardware issue.

Summary

Check Disk Management first, assign a drive letter, rebuild a RAW partition with TestDisk, and use a powered hub. Only if the drive clicks should you consider data recovery services.
